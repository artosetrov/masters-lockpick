What is Hacking Locks in Gothic Remake?

In the Gothic Remake, hacking locks is the chest-lockpicking minigame you meet whenever you find a locked chest or door without its key. Instead of a single tumbler, each lock is a stack of metal plates, and every plate holds a pin you must slide into the correct hole. When all pins line up on the goal hole (#4), the lock clicks open. This page is both a quick Gothic Remake lockpicking guide and a free solver — set up your lock at the top and it shows the exact moves.

How lockpicking works in Gothic Remake

Understanding the mechanics makes Gothic Remake lockpicking far less frustrating. Three things matter:

Plates and pins

Each lock has several plates (rows). On every plate a pin starts in some hole, and your goal is to bring all pins to the same target hole. Move a plate left or right to shift its pin.

Linked plates

Some plates are coupled: moving one also moves another, either the same way or the opposite way. These links are what make Gothic Remake lock combinations tricky — fixing one pin can knock another out of place.

Lockpick durability

Lockpicks wear out and snap. Untrained you get very few attempts per pick; training the skill gives more durability and forgiving feedback, so invest points early if you plan to open a lot of Gothic Remake locked chests.

Tips for opening locked chests

  • Train the lockpicking skill before tackling high-value Gothic Remake locked chests.
  • Work the most constrained pin first — the one closest to an edge has the least room to move.
  • Note the linked plates before you move anything; a single wrong direction can undo your progress.
  • Carry spare lockpicks; even with the right combination, a slip can cost a pick.
  • Use the solver to confirm a combination is actually reachable before brute-forcing a chest.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring couplings and treating every plate as independent.
  • Pushing a pin past the edge of the lock, which simply wastes a move.
  • Attempting hard chests untrained and burning through your whole lockpick stock.
  • Forgetting that an “opposite” link moves the partner plate the other direction.
